# Brand Context — 11 Onboarding Questions

Finalised 27 June 2026. These are the questions `crew-core-brand-context` asks. No visual design questions — those are gathered by design skills at build time or scraped from the website.

## The 11 questions

1. **What does your business do, and why does it matter?**
   Two sentences. What you make, sell, or provide. And why anyone should care.

2. **Who buys from you? Start with the person who pays. Then tell me about anyone else who matters.**
   Describe your primary customer. Then: anyone else who uses or influences the decision? Different age, different need, different problem. The contrasts matter as much as the common ground.

3. **Why would a customer leave you?**
   The real reason. What made someone walk away in the past? What do customers consistently misunderstand?

4. **If your business was a person at a dinner party, how would they show up?**
   What would they wear? How would they speak? Would they arrive early to help set up, slide in late and apologetic, or exactly on time with a bottle of wine? Warm host? Quiet expert? Loud storyteller? This is your voice.

5. **What's the one thing you always get right?**
   What do customers compliment? What do they tell their friends?

6. **What are you trying to achieve right now?**
   What does success look like in six months?

7. **What's your website? And where else do people find you online?**
   URL. Social accounts. Review pages. Anywhere a customer might comment or ask a question.

8. **What's something unwritten that a new person would need to know?**
   The unwritten rules. The way things actually get done. What would surprise an outsider.

9. **Where do you feel you let your customers down?**
   The gap between what you wish you could deliver and what you can reliably deliver today.

10. **Is there anything I must know to get this right?**
    Regulations. Sensitive topics. History that shapes decisions. Anything that would embarrass you if I got it wrong.

11. **What question haven't I asked that I should have?**
    The safety net. The thing that matters most that didn't fit.

## Design decisions

- **No visual design questions.** Colours, fonts, and style registers are gathered by design skills at build time or scraped from the website. A florist can onboard without thinking about fonts.
- **Dinner-party metaphor for voice.** More accessible than "tone of voice" or "brand personality." Anyone can answer how they'd show up.
- **Q11 is the safety net.** Every structured questionnaire misses something.
- **Arrival time in Q4.** Early/on-time/late says as much about a business as how they speak.
- **Merged "why leave" + "where let down"** — Q3 (external: what customers do), Q9 (internal: what you know is weak).

## Questions that were cut (and why)

- **"What have you tried before that worked? What didn't?"** — Jared: "I think that's a terrible question." Overlaps with Q3 and Q10.
- **Visual identity (colours, fonts, style, imagery)** — Too heavy for non-designers. Split to design skills.
- **"What's a promise you'd never make?"** — Reframed to Q9. The original invited platitudes.
