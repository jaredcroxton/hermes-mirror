# Psychosocial safety and Workhopper marketing research notes

Use when Jared asks for marketing research around psychological safety, psychosocial hazards, site safety, construction safety, or Workhopper/Tortoise & Hare positioning.

## Core angle
Psychosocial safety is now site safety. Position Workhopper as a site safety listening layer for psychosocial hazards, not as a generic wellness app.

Best framing:
- "The site safety risk your SWMS does not see."
- "Psychosocial hazards are now site safety."
- "Give every worker a safer way to speak up."
- "Workhopper helps site teams catch the risks people do not say at pre-start."

## Definitions to keep clean
- Psychological safety: workers feel safe to speak up, raise hazards, challenge unsafe decisions, admit mistakes, and ask for help.
- Psychosocial hazards: regulated WHS category. Work factors that can cause psychological or physical harm, including job demands, low control, poor support, role clarity, bullying, harassment, violence, traumatic events, poor physical environment, poor workplace relationships, and poor organisational justice.

Use "psychosocial hazards" for WHS, compliance, legal, and executive buyers. Use "safe to speak up" for site workers and supervisors. Use "psychological safety" as the bridge between them.

## Evidence-backed claims
- Safe Work Australia says PCBUs must manage psychosocial hazards under model WHS laws. Source: https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/safety-topic/managing-health-and-safety/mental-health/psychosocial-hazards
- Safe Work Australia 2025: mental health conditions accounted for 17,600 serious claims, 12% of all serious claims, in 2023-24p. Up 14.7% over one year and 161.1% over 10 years. Source: https://data.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/insights/key-whs-statistics-australia/latest-release
- Safe Work Australia 2025: mental health condition claims had 35.7 weeks median time lost and $67,400 median compensation paid. Almost five times median time lost and more than four times median compensation across all serious claims. Same source as above.
- Safe Work Australia 2025: mental stress caused 16,800 serious claims, 11.5% of serious claims. Main types: harassment/workplace bullying 33.2%, work pressure 24.2%, exposure to violence and harassment 15.7%.
- NSW Government 2025: psychological injury claims doubled in six years. Psychological claims are 12% of total workers compensation claims but 38% of total cost. Average claim cost rose from $146,000 in 2019-20 to $288,542 in 2024-25. Source: https://www.nsw.gov.au/ministerial-releases/workers-compensation-ministerial-statement
- SafeWork NSW Mental Health in Construction 2024: 2019 to 2024 had over 800 major workers compensation mental stress claims in construction, with work-related harassment and/or bullying the most common reason. Source: https://www.safework.nsw.gov.au/about-us/evaluation/findings-report-mental-health-in-construction-2024
- SafeWork NSW construction project: 426 sites visited, 272 notices issued, 24 improvement notices related to psychosocial risks, 77% of surveyed PCBUs/site supervisors said poor mental health is an issue, 38% had assisted a worker/colleague with mental health issues, 14% had nothing in place to communicate about psychosocial hazards, 22% had no adequate psychosocial control measures.
- SafeWork NSW construction guidance states: "Construction sites pose mental health risks, not just physical ones. Harassment and bullying lower morale. High pressure leads to burnout. Traumatic events leave lasting effects. Violence is a significant threat." Source: https://www.safework.nsw.gov.au/your-industry/construction/safety-topics/mental-health
- SafeWork NSW action plan lists construction psychosocial hazards including bullying/harassment, high workloads, traumatic events, hazardous physical working environments, being told to take safety shortcuts to "get the job done", conflict, and insufficient role clarity/instruction/training/supervision. Source: https://www.safework.nsw.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0006/1333653/action-plan-managing-psychosocial-risks-in-construction.pdf
- DCA 2023: 42% of Australian workers with poor mental health did not discuss their mental health with anyone at work in the previous 12 months. Source: https://www.dca.org.au/news/blog/addressing-workplace-mental-health
- National Safety Council: workers who feel their employer discourages reporting were 2.4 times more likely to have experienced a work injury; psychological safety may directly contribute to physical safety by encouraging proactive reporting. Source: https://www.nsc.org/workplace/workplace-wellbeing-hub/riREDACTED_API_KEY/wellbeing-foundations/psychological-safety-at-work

## Case bank
- Court Services Victoria, WorkSafe Victoria, 2023: convicted and fined $379,157 over toxic workplace culture at Coroners Court of Victoria. Risks included traumatic material, role conflict, high workloads, poor workplace relationships, bullying, intimidation, verbal abuse, inappropriate behaviours. Source: https://www.worksafe.vic.gov.au/news/2023-10/court-body-fined-almost-380000-deadly-work-culture
- Department of Defence, Comcare, 2025: convicted and fined $188,000 after failing to manage psychosocial risks relating to the death of a RAAF worker. First Commonwealth conviction of its kind. Source: https://www.comcare.gov.au/about/news-events/news/defence-convicted-after-raaf-workers-death
- Hunter tradesman, SafeWork NSW, 2019: convicted and fined $6,000 for bullying two young workers. Behaviour included verbal abuse, name calling, swearing, belittling and threats. Apprentices reported distress, anxiety, nightmares, fear of work. Source: https://www.safework.nsw.gov.au/news/safework-media-releases/tradesman-convicted-in-relation-to-bullying-young-workers
- UTS restructure, SafeWork NSW, 2025: prohibition notice paused redundancy consultation process due to psychological harm risk. Lifted after timelines and communications changed. Source: https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/nsw-safety-regulator-psychosocial-safety
- Western Sydney Local Health District, SafeWork NSW, 2025: landmark prosecution withdrawn after three weeks and 20 witnesses. Use as a grey-zone case showing stress alone is not automatically a breach; risk management and reasonable process matter. Source: https://www.ashurst.com/en/insights/safework-nsw-withdraws-landmark-psychosocial-risk-prosecution/
- WorkSafe WA Department of Justice, 2025: prosecution discontinued after fresh evidence. Use only to show regulator scrutiny, not liability. Source: https://www.worksafe.wa.gov.au/announcements/worksafe-discontinues-department-of-justice-prosecution-relation-bunbury-regional-prison
- Elisha v Vision Australia, High Court 2024: about $1.44m damages restored for psychiatric injury linked to breach of employment contract and termination process. Use as adjacent executive-risk case, not WHS prosecution. Source: https://www.hcourt.gov.au/cases-and-judgments/cases/decided/case-m222024

## Safe marketing language
Use:
- Helps identify psychosocial hazards
- Surfaces early warning signals
- Supports consultation and monitoring
- Helps leaders act earlier
- Creates anonymised site-level visibility
- Complements WHS systems
- Supports safer reporting

Avoid:
- Prevents psychological injury
- Stops claims
- Guarantees compliance
- Detects mental illness
- Diagnoses workers
- Protects you from prosecution
- Solves workplace mental health

## Content angles
- The next safety incident may start as silence.
- Psychosocial safety is not an HR project.
- The toolbox talk is not enough.
- Unsafe shortcuts are psychosocial hazards too.
- The apprentice who stops speaking up is the warning sign.

## Recommended offer language
"Psychosocial hazard intelligence for site safety."

The buyer does not wake up wanting wellness. They worry about incidents, premiums, claims, labour shortages, apprentices leaving, poor site culture, and regulators. Workhopper should make invisible safety risk visible without turning workers into case files.
