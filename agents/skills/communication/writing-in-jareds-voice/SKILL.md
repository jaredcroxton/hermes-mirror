# Writing in Jared's Voice

## When to use
- Jared asks Brock to draft an email he'll send as himself.
- Jared asks to review, humanise, or fix the tone of something he wrote or dictated.
- Any written comms where the byline is Jared, not PerformOS/the brand. (Brand copy has its own rules; this skill is personal comms.)

## Voice rules (non-negotiable — he corrected these)
- Casual and humanised, never formal or corporate. "Humanised" means it reads like a real person, not a memo. His first draft request will often be a voice-dictated ramble; clean up the fragments but keep the directness.
- Use contractions: don't, it's, I'd, I've, that's. Never "do not" / "it is" / "I would" / "I have".
- Short, punchy sentences. Active voice.
- Honesty and vulnerability are welcome. He will admit embarrassment, heavy personal usage, or "I don't have a clean estimate yet" — do not sanitise that into polished corporate hedging.
- No em dashes, ever. No brand jargon unless he names it himself.
- Plain "here's the reality" framing over "per my previous correspondence" framing.

## People (spell names correctly)
- Jayne Brown — VP Ops. It is "Jayne", not "Jane". REDACTED_EMAIL.
- Leanne Edwards — VP P&C.
- Chris Douglas.
- Confirm the recipient list and framing before finalising. He often widens the audience mid-draft (e.g. adds Leanne) or re-frames the purpose (e.g. "with budgets for next year").

## Sequencing an ask (prove first, ask later)
- When the end goal is money (recoup cost, performance pay), do NOT put the ask in the first email. Prove proof of concept first, then ask. Asking before the trial is weak; asking after the numbers move is strong.
- Tie any performance-pay ask to a metric the exec already owns (cost per hire, no-show rate, days to first sale), not "pay me for my time".
- If he wants licensing explicitly off the table, keep it off — position costing/recoup as the headline request instead.

## Pitfalls
- Don't over-flag after he's made a call. If he decides full transparency (e.g. sharing the raw build-cost figure, folding hardware + courses into the number), write it his way and drop the resistance. One flag, then defer.
- Don't treat his raw personal spend log as a scoped cost model — but if he explicitly wants the full number included, include it.
- Confirm name spellings before drafting (Jayne vs Jane cost a rewrite).
- Output as a Desktop markdown file, then present inline.
