# LearnOS cost email — worked example

Final version Jared approved. Tone: casual, honest, no money ask, framed as next-year budget context, sent to Jayne + Leanne.

---

**Subject:** LearnOS costs

Hi Jayne and Leanne,

I just thought I'd send this through with budgets for next year and around LearnOS.

To build it year to date it's cost me about $15,000. That includes my MIT Agentic AI course and a more powerful laptop to run the models, because the RAM and GPU needed to build and test it properly don't come free.

I don't have a locked cost for the next builds yet. Cloning the software for Member Care and Hotel Sales should be fairly straightforward, because the core is already built. Everything I've predicted with LearnOS has been correct so far, so my confidence in it is high. But I don't have an exact figure to share. It would land above the $15,000 setup cost.

The other side is the hours. I've used some form of AI every single day since the middle of March, except one day off. I talk to it instead of typing. That's the volume of work behind the scenes, and it's not simple. Getting it across to AWS on its own was a mission.

And new software will always have bugs I'll need to fix along the way.

So I don't have a clean estimate for you yet. Hopefully that gives you some context on what actually goes into it.

Jared

---

## Why it works (the corrections it reflects)
- First draft was too formal ("straight answer", "do not", "land that number properly"). Jared rejected it: "nah its not a right tone". Rewritten casual and plain.
- The $15K figure is stated plainly, with the MIT course + laptop folded in as legitimate build cost. This was Jared's explicit call after Brock flagged the raw spreadsheet was over-scoped — he wanted full transparency, so it stayed in.
- Honest "I don't have a clean estimate for you yet" — vulnerability over polish.
- No ask in this email. The recoup + performance-pay ask is held for after the telesales trial proves ROI (see SKILL.md sequencing).
- Contracted throughout (it's, I've, don't, that's, I'll). No em dashes.
