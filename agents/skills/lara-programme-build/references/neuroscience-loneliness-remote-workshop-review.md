# Neuroscience loneliness remote-workshop review

Session context: Jared developed a PSY6835 Assessment 2 workshop concept on loneliness among people working from home. Audience was managers of remote/hybrid employees. This reference captures the reusable review pattern and evidence notes for future small academic learning-design checks.

## Best topic framing

Use: **The Lonely Brain at Work: How Remote Isolation Affects Stress Biology and What Managers Can Do**

Best target audience: **Managers of remote and hybrid employees in professional workplaces**. This is stronger than targeting remote workers generally because managers can change team routines, check-in practices, and psychological safety.

## Activity that worked

**The Connection Audit, 10 minutes**

Managers privately list direct reports, mark the last time they had a genuine human conversation with each person, circle people they would notice going quiet, and place a question mark next to anyone they are unsure about. Debrief lightly with: "What surprised you?" Do not ask for names or disclosure.

Bridge line:

> Hold that question mark. We are now going to look at what may be happening in the brain and body of someone who feels socially disconnected, even when they are still attending meetings and getting work done.

## Academic wording adjustments

Prefer precise neuroscience language:

- Use: "Perceived isolation can heighten threat-detection systems, including amygdala-linked vigilance, and activate the HPA axis."
- Avoid: "the amygdala fires"
- Use: "When this response becomes chronic, the normal cortisol feedback loop can become dysregulated."
- Avoid: "cortisol floods"
- Frame manager action as **stress buffering**, not curing loneliness.
- Add: "The aim is not to diagnose loneliness from silence. The aim is to notice patterns and open low-pressure connection."

## Cultural safety adaptation

In higher face-sensitivity or collectivist contexts, begin with a third-person vignette rather than a personal audit. This reduces risk of public shame and lets managers discuss disconnection safely before reflecting on their own team.

## Evidence notes

Peer-reviewed evidence supports this pathway:

**Perceived social isolation → brain interprets social environment as less safe → threat vigilance/HPA activation → cortisol rhythm dysregulation → immune/inflammatory and health impacts → changes in cognition, emotion, sleep, and connection.**

Useful sources found:

- Cacioppo, J. T., Cacioppo, S., Capitanio, J. P., & Cole, S. W. (2015/2014 final form). *The neuroendocrinology of social isolation*. Annual Review of Psychology, 66, 733–767. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-010814-015240
  - Key use: perceived social isolation matters because the brain interprets the social environment. Chronic loneliness activates neuroendocrine and behavioural self-preservation systems with long-term health costs.
- Doane, L. D., & Adam, E. K. (2010). *Loneliness and cortisol: Momentary, day-to-day, and trait associations*. Psychoneuroendocrinology, 35(3), 430–441. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psyneuen.2009.08.005
  - Key use: trait loneliness predicted flatter diurnal cortisol rhythms; prior-day loneliness predicted larger cortisol awakening response; momentary loneliness predicted cortisol increases under high chronic interpersonal stress.
- Figueiredo, E., Margaça, C., & Sánchez-García, J. C. (2025). *Loneliness and isolation in the era of telework: A comprehensive review of challenges for organizational success*. Healthcare, 13(16), 1943. https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare13161943
  - Key use: telework can reduce informal interaction, weaken belonging, and intensify loneliness, social/professional isolation, technostress, burnout, and productivity challenges.
- Gouin, J.-P., & Kiecolt-Glaser, J. K. (2012). *The impact of psychological stress on wound healing: Methods and mechanisms*. Critical Care Nursing Clinics of North America, 24(2), 201–213. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccell.2012.03.006
  - Key use from Sam notes: psychological stress impairs wound repair through cortisol, catecholamines, local cytokines, MMP activity, leukocyte infiltration, infection susceptibility, and behavioural pathways. Social support and oxytocin are protective.

## Best Part 2 resource choice

Use a **double-sided manager handout**, not slides, for a small build. Suggested title: **Remote Connection Check: A Manager’s Guide to the Lonely Brain**.

Side one:
- What loneliness is
- Why the brain treats social isolation as threat
- Visual process: isolation → threat detection → HPA axis → cortisol dysregulation → body/behaviour effects

Side two:
- Spot the signs
- What not to assume
- Three manager actions
- One reflection prompt
- APA reference list
