# Brand Context — The 11 Onboarding Questions

Designed 25 June 2026 after rejecting the original 12-question set. The original questions were too visual-design-heavy for a business owner. These require no design vocabulary. Every question has a purpose.

## The questions

1. **What does your business do, and why does it matter?**
   Two sentences. What you make, sell, or provide. And why anyone should care. No jargon.

2. **Who buys from you? Start with the person who pays. Then tell me about anyone else who matters.**
   Describe your primary customer — the one who pays. Real person. What do they care about? Then: does anyone else use or influence the decision? Different age group? Different need? Different problem? The contrasts matter as much as the common ground.

3. **Why would a customer leave you?**
   The real reason. What made someone walk away in the past? What do customers consistently misunderstand?

4. **If your business was a person at a dinner party, how would they show up?**
   What would they wear? How would they speak? Would they arrive early to help set up, slide in late and apologetic, or exactly on time with a bottle of wine? Warm host? Quiet expert? Loud storyteller? This is your voice.

5. **What's the one thing you always get right?**
   What do customers compliment? What do they tell their friends?

6. **What are you trying to achieve right now?**
   What does success look like in six months? One measurable outcome.

7. **What's your website? And where else do people find you online?**
   URL. Social accounts. Review pages. Anywhere a customer might comment or ask a question.

8. **What's something unwritten that a new person would need to know?**
   The unwritten rules. The way things actually get done. What would surprise an outsider.

9. **Where do you feel you let your customers down?**
    The gap between what you wish you could deliver and what you can reliably deliver today. The thing you've learned not to promise because you've been burned before. Not where you failed — where you fall short.

10. **Is there anything I must know to get this right?**
    Regulations. Sensitive topics. Company history that shapes decisions. Anything that would embarrass you if I got it wrong.

11. **What question haven't I asked that I should have?**
    The safety net. The thing that matters most that didn't fit the other ten.

## Why these work

- **No design vocabulary required.** Q4 replaces the old "visual style" question. A business owner can describe how they show up at a dinner party. The skill translates that into a design register.
- **Every question has a purpose.** Q2→audience segments, Q3→pain points and churn, Q4→voice, Q5→strengths, Q7→source material for content skills, Q8→nuance and unwritten culture, Q9→guardrails and integrity
- **They build trust.** Q8 and Q10 signal you care about the real culture and what's at stake, not just the marketing copy.
- **Q11 is the catch-all.** It surfaces what no structured questionnaire can predict. Often the most valuable answer.

**What was rejected, and how each question evolved (25 June 2026 session):**

- **Original 12 design-heavy questions (24 June).** Included colours, heading font, body font, visual style register, imagery style, and "what have you tried before." Rejected because non-designers cannot answer them. Design specifics are gathered by individual build skills or scraped from the website, not during brand onboarding.
- **Q4 evolution.** Original: "What visual style?" Replaced with the dinner party metaphor. Then Jared added the arrival timing dimension (early to help, late and apologetic, on time with wine). How a business handles time says as much as how they speak.
- **Q9 evolution.** Original: "What's a promise you'd never make to a customer? Even if it cost you the sale." Jared reframed: "Where do you feel you let your customers down?" The new framing surfaces the gap between aspiration and reality, not just the integrity line.
- **Q10 (original).** "What have you tried before that worked? What didn't?" Jared rejected: "Terrible question." Overlapped with Q3 and Q10. Cut.
- **Q12 merged into Q10.** The original two catch-all questions ("anything I must know" + "what haven't I asked") merged into Q10 and Q11 — one for must-know, one for the safety net.
