## Claims Supported

**Claim A — Melatonin suppression: CONFIRMED**

Quote from results: "The LE-e-book condition suppressed evening levels of melatonin by 55.12 ± 20.12%, whereas the print-book condition showed no suppression (−18.77 ± 39.57%)" (P < 0.001).

**Claim B — Sleep onset delay: CONFIRMED**

Quote from significance statement: "We found that the use of these devices before bedtime prolongs the time it takes to fall asleep"

Sleep latency: 25.65 ± 18.78 min (LE-eBook) vs 15.75 ± 13.09 min (printed book), P = 0.009.

## Study Design Summary

- 12 healthy young adults (mean age 24.9 y; 6 women)
- 14-day inpatient randomised crossover protocol
- 5 consecutive evenings reading LE-eBook (iPad, max brightness) vs printed book
- 4 hours of reading before scheduled bedtime (18:00–22:00)
- Dim ambient room light (~3 lux) in both conditions
- Outcomes: plasma melatonin, polysomnography, subjective sleepiness (KSS), waking EEG

## Typical Use

This paper directly supports the claim: "Evening exposure to screen light suppresses melatonin secretion and delays sleep onset." It is the strongest single-reference choice for a neuroscience assignment on blue-light/screen-technology cessation because it measures both outcomes in the same protocol using gold-standard inpatient circadian methodology.
