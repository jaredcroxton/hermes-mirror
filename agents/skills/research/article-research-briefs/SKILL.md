# Article Research Briefs

## When to use
- Jared asks for “deep research” for an article, post, speech, newsletter, or thought piece.
- Jared provides one or more source links and asks what else has happened in the same space.
- The task needs live examples, credible sources, article angles, or a research spine rather than a generic summary.

## Output standard
- Give Jared the **article spine**, not a dump of links.
- Lead with the core angle in plain language.
- Separate:
  1. clean, direct examples
  2. adjacent or grey-zone examples
  3. wider context and governance failures
  4. lines Jared can use in the article
- Be clear where causation is proven versus inferred. Use language like “direct backtrack”, “AI-linked”, “company denies the AI link”, or “adjacent governance failure”.
- Prefer Australian examples first when Jared asks from an Australian lens. Use global examples only as comparators.
- Include source URLs under each case.
- Avoid academic tone. Write in Jared’s short, punchy style.

## Research workflow
1. Extract the user-provided source links first. Capture dates, organisation, what happened, numbers, quotes, and why it matters.
2. Run broad web searches for comparable incidents. Use source-quality filters where possible: ABC, Guardian Australia, Reuters, BBC, OAIC, ANAO, official regulator pages, government pages, credible union statements.
3. For each candidate case, verify with a primary or reputable source before including it as a fact.
4. Sort cases by article usefulness, not by discovery order.
5. Name the pattern. Jared needs the argument, not just examples.
6. Finish with sharp lines or thesis options he can lift directly.

## Pitfalls
- Do not present a company denial as proof that AI was unrelated. Phrase it as a disputed or grey-zone case.
- Do not over-claim “rehiring” when the evidence only shows a pause, shutdown, redeployment, or resumed hiring.
- Do not treat AI as the villain by default. The sharper analysis is usually premature operating-model change, weak governance, or leaders banking efficiency before the work has truly disappeared.
- Do not bury the most useful examples under policy context. Start with the cases that make the article work.

## Pricing-claim research pattern
- When researching model pricing claims, separate base/sticker token pricing from effective workload cost, cache reads, batch discounts, and cost-per-benchmark-task claims.
- Search exact claim strings first, including quoted phrases such as “50% cheaper”, “half the cost”, “up to 45%”, and “75% cheaper cache reads”.
- Classify each source as accurate, misleading, or adjacent. Adjacent means the claim is true for a related model or comparison but not the user's target claim.
- Treat social snippets as leads only. Do not use them as authoritative proof unless the page itself can be accessed and the claim can be quoted.
