# Leadership Agent Research Pattern

Use when Jared asks to build a leadership, executive judgement, difficult-conversation, or manager-coaching specialist.

## Session learning

Jared corrected a first-pass leadership answer because it over-indexed on authority. His requirement was not just a firmer script. He wanted a leadership agent that can take rich team context, research high-end leadership thinkers, and return clear strategic stances.

The durable lesson: leadership advice for Jared must not default to authority, empathy, or scripts alone. It must diagnose the situation, present multiple strategic angles, and recommend a stance.

## Source-backed leadership lenses

### Brené Brown: clarity plus vulnerability

Source: https://brenebrown.com/articles/2018/10/15/clear-is-kind-unclear-is-unkind/

Useful principles:
- "Clear is kind. Unclear is unkind."
- Avoiding tough conversations reduces trust, engagement, clarity, and performance.
- A "rumble" means staying curious, owning your part, staying in the messy middle, and listening with the same passion you want to be heard.

How to apply:
- Do not make hard conversations ego-based.
- Name the impact clearly.
- Ask what needs to be understood before judging intent.

Example move:
> "I want to understand what happened, but I also want to be clear about the impact."

### Simon Sinek: safety before authority

Source: https://www.ted.com/talks/simon_sinek_why_good_leaders_make_you_feel_safe

Useful principles:
- Good leaders make people feel secure.
- Trust and safety create the conditions for cooperation and truth-telling.
- If people feel threatened, they protect themselves.

How to apply:
- Treat resistance as data before treating it as defiance.
- Ask whether the behaviour signals avoidance, uncertainty, safety-seeking, politics, or capability gaps.
- Hold boundaries without humiliation.

Example move:
> "Help me understand what you were hoping that would add."

### Barack Obama: listen broadly, decide clearly

Source: https://barackobama.medium.com/how-i-approach-the-toughest-decisions-dc1b165cdf2d

Useful principles:
- Listen to experts, follow facts, weigh goals against principles.
- Ask different voices, including junior or contrarian voices.
- Create space to think, then decide.

How to apply:
- Do not confuse input with permission.
- Invite views before setting structure.
- Make the decision cleanly after listening.

Example move:
> "I want your view before I decide how we structure this moving forward."

### Amy Edmondson: psychological safety is not softness

Source: https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/four-steps-to-build-the-psychological-safety-that-high-performing-teams-need-today

Useful principles:
- Psychological safety means candor is expected and people can speak up without fear of retribution.
- It is not avoiding standards, disagreement, or accountability.
- Leaders should seek input with humility and openness, and not punish bad news.

How to apply:
- Pair honesty with standards.
- Make it safe to explain intent.
- Keep accountability intact.

Example move:
> "I want you to feel safe being honest with me. I also need direct accountability."

### Radical Candor: care personally, challenge directly

Source: https://www.radicalcandor.com/our-approach

Useful principles:
- Effective feedback combines Care Personally and Challenge Directly.
- Challenge without care becomes obnoxious aggression.
- Care without challenge becomes ruinous empathy.

How to apply:
- Start with relationship and intent.
- Then name the standard and impact.

Example move:
> "I value the working relationship, which is why I need to be clear about the impact."

### Center for Creative Leadership: SBII

Source: https://www.ccl.org/articles/leading-effectively-articles/closing-the-gap-between-intent-vs-impact-sbii/

Useful principles:
- Situation: name the moment.
- Behaviour: name observable action.
- Impact: name the effect.
- Intent: ask what was behind it.

How to apply:
- Use SBII when a manager no-shows, dilutes a meeting, pushes back, interrupts, avoids accountability, or sends a proxy.

Template:
> "In [situation], I noticed [behaviour]. The impact was [impact]. What was happening from your side?"

## Required output shape for leadership-agent advice

When designing or operating a leadership specialist for Jared, produce:
1. Leadership diagnosis
2. Three to five strategic angles
3. Recommended stance
4. Exact words Jared can use
5. What not to say
6. Risks and watchouts
7. Follow-up move

## Common strategic angles

- Authority and role clarity: useful for accountability and decision rights. Risk: compliance without trust.
- Psychological safety and curiosity: useful for uncertainty, fear, or politics. Risk: sounding weak if no boundary follows.
- Radical Candor: useful when relationship and challenge both matter. Risk: sounding like corporate therapy.
- Executive decision process: useful when input matters but Jared owns the decision. Risk: people confuse input with permission.
- Political and reputational risk: useful during role transition or promotion moments. Risk: overplaying status and looking insecure.

## Pitfalls

- Do not give only one script.
- Do not default to authority as the first and only stance.
- Do not assume resistance equals disrespect.
- Do not collapse psychological safety into softness.
- Do not let empathy remove the boundary.
- Do not create a specialist soul until Jared decides whether it is a direct specialist bot or a Brock-routed specialist.
