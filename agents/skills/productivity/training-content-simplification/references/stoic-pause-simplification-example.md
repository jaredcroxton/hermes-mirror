# Stoic Pause - Simplification Example

Source: Expert content from stoic-mindset-session.xlsx (Module 3, Column D)
Session: 28 May 2026

## Original (Expert Language)

- Seneca's premeditatio malorum: mentally rehearsing adversity before it happens to strip it of its emotional power
- Victor Frankl's insight (stoic-adjacent): "Between stimulus and response there is a space. In that space is our power to choose our response."
- Mary Helen Immordino-Yang: emotion is not the enemy of rationality -- emotion is the biological prerequisite for encoding meaning. The goal is not to suppress emotion but to integrate it with cognition
- John Medina Rule 4 (attention) and Rule 8 (stress): the pause reclaims attention from the amygdala and restores prefrontal function
- The physiological mechanics: 3 slow breaths activate the parasympathetic nervous system, shifting the brain from threat-detection to executive function mode (Huberman: physiological sigh)

## Simplified (Plain English for Sales Reps)

- **The Stoic Pause:** When something triggers you (angry email, harsh feedback, a surprise problem), do not react straight away. Pause. Take three slow breaths. Then respond.
- **Why it works:** Your emotional brain reacts in under half a second. Your thinking brain takes two to three seconds to catch up. The pause gives your thinking brain time to show up.
- **Emotion is not the enemy:** You need it. But you do not have to let it drive. Feel it. Then choose your response.
- **The three breaths:** In through the nose, a second sip of air, then a long slow exhale. This is the fastest way to calm your body and clear your head. Do it before you hit reply.
- **Victor Frankl gap:** Between what happens to you and how you respond, there is a space. In that space is your power. The pause is how you widen that space.
