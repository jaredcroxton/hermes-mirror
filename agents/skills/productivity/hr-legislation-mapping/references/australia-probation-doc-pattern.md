# Australia probation termination document pattern

Session-derived pattern validated by Jared for HR/employment-legislation mapping documents.

## Required top order

After the title/status/purpose note and `Incident / question`, put the practical sections first:

1. `What this means you may be able to do`
2. `To proceed, what would need to be done`
3. `Risks to cross off before acting`
4. `Legislation and source`
5. `Verification note`

Jared specifically preferred this over starting with dense legislation. The document should help him understand the practical shape before going into business conversations, while still preserving the source-backed legal basis below.

## Example bullets: What this means you may be able to do

- The source indicates probation can be used to assess whether the employee is suitable for the role.
- The source suggests a probation termination can proceed if the employee has not passed probation, but minimum entitlements still apply.
- For one year or less continuous service, the Fair Work minimum notice table indicates one week of notice, worked or paid in lieu.
- The reason should be framed factually around suitability, performance, conduct, and team impact, not vague culture-fit language alone.

## Example bullets: To proceed, what would need to be done

- Confirm the employee's length of service and whether they are still inside the contractual probation period.
- Confirm employment type, award or enterprise agreement coverage, and the employment contract notice clause.
- Confirm whether company policy requires warnings, a probation review meeting, a support period, or HR approval before termination.
- Document the specific performance, conduct, and team-impact concerns in factual language, with examples.
- Tell the employee the reason and give them a chance to respond, especially if the reason includes performance or conduct.
- If proceeding, provide written notice or payment in lieu and calculate final pay, including wages owing and unused accumulated annual leave.
- Have the business HR expert review the proposed reason, process, letter, notice, and final pay before any conversation occurs.

## Example bullets: Risks to cross off before acting

- General protections or adverse action risk, for example if the employee has exercised a workplace right, made a complaint, taken leave, or raised a concern.
- Discrimination risk, including any protected attribute, illness, injury, disability, pregnancy, carer responsibility, age, race, sex, or other protected ground.
- Procedural fairness risk if the employee has not been told the concern or given a chance to respond.
- Documentation risk if the concerns are vague, unsupported, or based on subjective culture-fit language rather than specific examples.
- Contract, award, enterprise agreement, or policy risk if any of those provide more generous notice or a required internal process.
- Retaliation or timing risk if the termination follows a complaint, leave request, injury, conflict, or protected activity.
- Final pay risk if wages, leave, loading, allowances, or payment timing are not checked before action.

## Legislation section pattern

For each legal point, keep these together:

- heading for the point, e.g. `Notice during probation`
- source name and official domain
- direct link
- verbatim quote
- plain-English meaning

Do not split source, quote, link, and mapping into separate tables unless Jared explicitly asks for an evidence table.
