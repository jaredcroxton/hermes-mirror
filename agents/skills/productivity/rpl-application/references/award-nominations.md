# Award and Scholarship Nomination Builder

## When to use

Load this reference when:
- Jared asks about a university award, scholarship, or prize
- He asks "can I apply for this?" or "is there anything here for me?"
- He needs a nomination drafted for submission

## Workflow

### Phase 1: Research and Fit Assessment

1. **Extract criteria.** Pull the award webpage content. If the PDF guide is binary (read_file fails), fall back to `pdftotext` via terminal. Do not skip this step — the guide often contains evaluation rules not visible on the webpage.

2. **Cross-reference every category against Jared's context.** Use memory (professional role, side venture, study), Obsidian study notes (course content, theories, grades), and agent ecosystem context. Be honest about fit. Tell him when a category is ineligible or weak. Never cheerlead a bad fit.

3. **Identify the best category.** Nominate one. Explain why the others don't fit. Be specific.

### Phase 2: Drafting

1. **Person is critical.** Self-nominations: FIRST PERSON ("I"). Third-party nominations: THIRD PERSON ("Jared"). Ask which it is before drafting. Do not assume.

2. **Embed specific course content.** Panels want evidence the student applied what they learned, not just that they got promoted. For each subject referenced:
   - Name one specific theory, model, or framework from the course
   - Cite one key author/researcher (e.g. Bakker & Demerouti 2007, Edmondson 1999)
   - Show HOW it was applied to real work, not just that it was studied

3. **Match the evaluation criteria.** University awards typically assess Depth, Breadth, Sustainability, and Relevance of impact. Every section should speak to these, explicitly or implicitly.

4. **Handle the paid-role rule.** Most award guides say contributions must go "beyond reasonably expected paid work." State this explicitly in the draft. Frame the contribution as what was done above and beyond the role.

### Phase 3: Quality Gates

1. **No em dashes.** Ever. In any public-facing copy. Replace with commas, colons, or sentence breaks.

2. **Word counts.** Award nomination forms have hard word limits (e.g. 500 words summary, 200 words evidence). Check every section.

3. **Supporting documents.** Most awards allow 4 pages of attachments. Identify what Jared can provide: transcript, certificates, referee letters, event materials.

4. **Endorsement requirement.** Check if a Dean or Director must endorse. If yes, flag this as the critical path item. It is usually the bottleneck.

5. **Grades are evidence.** If the transcript shows grades, use them. "Four subjects with grades of High Distinction and Distinction" is stronger than "completed four subjects."

## Pitfalls

1. **Writing a third-person draft for a self-nomination.** If the user is self-nominating, write "I". Do not write "Jared" or "the nominee."
2. **Vague course references.** "Applied wellbeing principles" is weak. "Applied Bakker and Demerouti's (2007) JD-R model" is strong. Name the theory.
3. **Skipping the nomination guide PDF.** The webpage summary often omits evaluation details. Always extract the PDF guide.
4. **Cheerleading a bad fit.** If a category doesn't fit, say so. Don't soften it. Jared moves fast and wants the honest answer.
5. **Forgetting the supporting documents.** The nomination text is half the job. The other half is identifying what evidence gets attached.
