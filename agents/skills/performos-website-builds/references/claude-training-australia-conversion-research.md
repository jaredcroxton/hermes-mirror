## Conversion patterns that matter

Strong pages usually include:

1. A clear buyer identity: business teams, marketers, developers, beginners, leaders.
2. A visible outcome: build workflows, automate work, improve productivity, create assets, build apps.
3. A low-friction next step: discovery call, choose a course, request callback, pick a date.
4. Specificity: duration, price, dates, tools, what participants will build.
5. Trust: testimonials, number trained, certificate, provider credibility, instructor credibility, and honest fit/not-fit copy.

## Positioning recommendation

Do not position the page as generic AI training. The market is full of one-day prompt courses, university short courses, and broad ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini workshops.

Position PerformOS training as:

> Learn to build real AI systems with Claude Code. Pick the path that matches what you want to ship.

Anchor line:

> Choose what you want to build. We teach you the AI system behind it.

This avoids competing directly with cheap prompt courses and free Anthropic material. It sells practical build outcomes.

## Homepage implication

Keep the homepage simple with three cards only. Sharpen the hero and card labels so each is a clear offer.

Suggested hero:

> Private AI systems for businesses that need more leverage.

Suggested subhead:

> We build practical AI agents, business tools, and training programmes that help teams automate work, make better decisions, and ship faster.

Suggested three-card labels:

1. AI Instruments — buy once, own forever; practical tools for leads, dashboards, reporting, and operations.
2. Private AI Agents — a managed AI team built around business data, workflows, and approvals.
3. AI Capability Sprints — modular training that teaches people to build, automate, review, and ship real work with AI.

## Training page implication

The current training page has strong visual style but three conversion issues:

1. The path choice sits too far below the fold because the hero is too tall.
2. It promises three paths but does not reveal enough module proof immediately.
3. The tool-chip list can overwhelm beginners if shown before the buyer has chosen a path.

Recommended first-screen job:

> What do you want to learn to build?

Then show the three paths immediately.

## Recommended dropdown model

Do not make the dropdown start as a module selector. Buyers do not think in modules first. They think in outcomes.

Use a dropdown labelled:

> What do you want to learn?

Suggested options:

- I want to build websites with Claude Code
- I want to build apps and dashboards
- I want to automate my business
- I want to build AI agents
- I want to train my team
- I want to understand governance and safe deployment
- I am not sure yet

On selection, reveal:

1. recommended path
2. relevant modules
3. example projects
4. best CTA

## Recommended page structure

1. Hero: Learn to build with AI. Choose what you want to ship.
2. Microproof: 10 modules · 65+ lessons · real projects · everything you build is yours to keep.
3. Three path cards above the fold: Build Yourself, Build for Small Business, Build for Enterprise.
4. Outcome dropdown: What do you want to learn?
5. Shared foundation modules 1 to 7.
6. Forked modules 8 to 10 by path.
7. Trust section: instructor/operator credibility, certificate, examples, fit/not-fit copy.
8. CTA per path plus a small "not sure where to start" CTA.
