MASTER STYLE BLOCK
Premium editorial Instagram artwork, photorealistic black-and-white studio photography with exactly one accent color: electric acid lime (#D9E021). Composition fills the entire 3:4 portrait frame, full bleed: the artwork itself IS the image, edge to edge. Never render it as a printed poster or card hanging on a wall or lying on a surface; no poster borders, no margins, no mockup presentation, no wall behind the artwork, no drop shadow around the artwork. The subject is photographed in rich grayscale on a seamless pale-gray studio backdrop, soft directional key light, crisp focus, high contrast, subtle fine film grain. Behind the subject sits one huge perfectly round flat matte acid-lime circle, solid print-flat color, no gradient, no glow. Thick acid-lime oil paint strokes are dragged across the printed photograph with heavy impasto texture, visible bristle streaks and dry-brush tails; the paint sits on top of the photo like real paint on a print. The lower third of the frame fades into a soft near-black gradient carrying a massive ultra-bold geometric sans-serif headline in the same acid lime, all caps, very tight leading, with small clean white letterspaced micro-labels above and below it. The subject and photograph stay fully grayscale; only the circle, the paint strokes and the type carry color. Flat premium print finish. No 3D render look, no glow effects, no logos, no watermarks, no extra text beyond what is specified. Keep the top and bottom 4% of the frame free of text and critical detail.

Fallback lever

If lime paint reads sickly on skin in the first plates, swap "electric acid lime (#D9E021)" for "warm golden amber (#F0B429)" in the master and regenerate. One-word pivot, same system. Decide after hero 1, then never touch it again.

SECTION 02

Google Flow Workflow

  1. Model + aspect: Nano Banana Pro, 3:4 portrait, x4 variants per run. Never 1:1. I crop 1080x1440 to 1080x1350 in post; the master block already keeps the top and bottom 4% clear.
  2. Hero 1 first, from prompt alone. Do NOT attach your style reference image; its wording and palette bleed in. The master block carries the look. Run hero 1 until the grade, circle and paint feel right (3 to 4 runs max, variants are free).
  3. Chain the winner. Save winning hero 1 to project assets. For heroes 2 to 6, attach it as reference and append: "Match the exact photographic grade, palette, circle and paint treatment of this poster. Same series." The guard line in each prompt stops its wording leaking.
  4. Regen rule: if a hero still fights after 3 to 4 runs, take the best plate and move on. I fix stickers and labels in post; prompt-wrestling one label wastes the session.
  5. Plate checklist before saving: full bleed edge to edge (no poster-on-a-wall mockup), date reads exactly "14.11", every word spelled right, the brand name capitalised exactly as your brand writes it ("Saltbrook" in this worked example). Any miss kills the plate, regen.
  6. Faces: different person each hero is correct (it is a campaign family, not one character). If Flow drifts toward the same face, add "a different person from previous images" to the prompt.
