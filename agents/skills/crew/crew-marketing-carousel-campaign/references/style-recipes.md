# Style Recipes: Master Style Blocks

The master style block is the whole look in one paragraph. It is auto-prefixed to every hero prompt so each copy is one paste-ready prompt. Three proven recipes below, plus how to derive a new one from a reference image.

Every master block MUST contain, verbatim in spirit:
- the aspect + full-bleed law: "fills the entire 3:4 portrait frame, full bleed: the artwork itself IS the image, edge to edge. Never render it as a printed poster or card hanging on a wall or lying on a surface; no poster borders, no margins, no mockup, no drop shadow."
- the top/bottom safety: "Keep the top and bottom 4% of the frame free of text and critical detail." (You will crop 1080x1440 to 1080x1350.)
- a single, named accent colour used consistently.

And every hero's TYPE block MUST start with the guard line:
`TYPE & LABELS (ignore all wording in any attached reference image, use only the wording below): ...`

## Recipe A: THE ACID ARCHIVE (brutalist acid-editorial)

The original. Aged newsprint, marble statues eroding into pixel-sort, acid-lime stickers stamped over the artwork, hand-scrawled marker, technical marginalia (wireframe globe, crop marks, registration targets, EAN digits, dotted fold lines), one lime square sticker with a bold up-right arrow. Ink black + acid lime (#D9E021) on off-white paper. Halftone texture, flat inks, photocopy grain, no photographic lighting, no glossy 3D.

Subject device per carousel = a marble-carved work tool glitching (megaphone = marketing, handshake = sales, gears = ops, abacus = finance, bust+headset = support). Hook slide = a marble head dissolving into falling data.

Best for: challenger/tech brands, "the old way is dead" angles, anything that should feel handmade and anti-corporate.

---

## Recipe B: LIMELIGHT (grayscale photoreal + one accent)

Photorealistic black-and-white studio portrait on a seamless pale-gray backdrop, soft directional key light, fine film grain. Behind the subject: one huge perfectly round FLAT MATTE acid-lime (#D9E021) circle (no gradient, no glow). Thick acid-lime oil-paint strokes dragged across the printed photo with heavy impasto, visible bristle streaks, dry-brush tails, paint sitting ON the photo. Lower third fades to near-black carrying a massive ultra-bold geometric sans headline in the same lime, all caps, tight leading, with small white letterspaced micro-labels above/below. Subject stays fully grayscale; only circle + paint + type carry colour.

The paint MEANS something per carousel (this is the engine): war paint on cheekbones (announcement), a blindfold across the eyes (differentiator), tally marks on the jaw (credentials), a swarm of lime squares above an open palm (mechanism), one cheek stroke on a laughing owner (close), a mirror-silhouette "painted twin" head on the circle (signature angle).

Fallback lever: if lime reads sickly on skin in plate 1, swap "electric acid lime (#D9E021)" for "warm golden amber (#F0B429)" once, then never touch it again.

Best for: premium, human, credibility-led campaigns. Highest scroll-stop (photoreal faces + one accent).

## Recipe C: THE DROP (product-drop, the offer made physical)

Glossy fashion-campaign photography, colour-drenched monochrome studio room: walls and ceiling in ONE saturated colour, checkerboard floor alternating that colour and white. Wide-angle low camera, crisp studio flash, editorial grade, film grain, real skin texture. Model in minimal ivory + black streetwear, white sneakers. The hero object is THE OFFER MADE PHYSICAL: a premium glossy object with softly glowing electric acid-lime (#D9E021) light and crisp printed label type, held out to camera / huge in the foreground. Massive ultra-bold sans headline in the largest empty wall area (never over the object or person), white on dark rooms / ink on light rooms, with small letterspaced micro-labels.

Family glue vs variety: room colour ROTATES per carousel (lime, powder blue, terracotta, butter yellow, warm cream, near-black) so each week's post looks distinct; the glowing lime object + ivory/black wardrobe + checkerboard floor are constant. Object labels carry real words from the offer (the offer name, one word per tile) because product-label type is what this model renders best. Never ask for real brand logos, label text only.

Object devices: a giant black slab with a glowing window (announcement), a glowing tile held out while dusty blank binders droop in the other hand (differentiator), three tiles fanned like cards each with one credential word (credentials), an open presentation case of dozens of glowing chips (mechanism), an apron-wearing owner holding the tile like it weighs nothing (close), a levitating cube carrying the offer's key word as the sole light source in a near-black room (signature angle).

Best for: launches, product energy, "own it" ownership angles. Pairs with a 4K upgrade pass (`regen_4k.py`) beautifully.

## Deriving a NEW recipe from a reference image

1. Name the ONE structural device that stops the scroll (the paint smear, the lime circle, the colour-drenched room, the checkerboard floor + held object). Steal that, not the surface.
2. Name the ONE accent colour and map it to the brand: take the brand's own accent from brand-context; the reference's own accent gets replaced.
3. Make the device SEMANTIC, it must mean something different on each of the six carousels, not be decoration. This is what separates a campaign from six posters.
4. Grayscale-or-monochrome + one accent is the reliable scroll-stop formula. Photoreal human faces are the highest-stopping subject class on Meta.
5. Write the master block covering: medium/grade, the device, the accent rule, the type treatment (big display only on heroes), and the full-bleed law + top/bottom safety.
6. Keep 3 text elements max on a hero: kicker, headline, subline.
