# Safe Claims — Exact Approved Wording

Every claim that references a third-party certification must use these exact words or a close variant that passes the same tests. No ad-libbing.

---

## Course sales page — approved wording

### Instructor qualifications block

> "Your instructor, Jared Croxton, holds current Microsoft Azure AI certifications — AI-900: Azure AI Fundamentals and AI-102: Azure AI Engineer Associate. These credentials inform the course curriculum and practical exercises. Real certifications. Real teaching. Not a chatbot."

Placement: Instructor section, body text. Never in hero H1.

### Course description block

> "This 12-week personalised AI course is designed and delivered by PerformOS. It draws on the instructor's industry-recognised certifications in Microsoft Azure AI and practical AI tooling to give you hands-on skills you can apply immediately."

Placement: Any supporting copy on the page. Never in hero.

### Mandatory negative disclosure (on sales page, below pricing)

> "This course is not a Microsoft, OpenAI, or ChatGPT certification program. Completion does not confer any Microsoft, OpenAI, or third-party credential. PerformOS is an independent training provider and is not endorsed by, affiliated with, or partnered with Microsoft Corporation, OpenAI, or any certification-issuing body. Your instructor holds current Microsoft Azure AI certifications (AI-900 and AI-102) as independent professional credentials. Those credentials inform the curriculum but do not make this an accredited Microsoft course. You receive a PerformOS Certificate of Completion."

### Pricing block

> "One-time payment of $499 AUD. Includes 12 weeks of personalised instruction, all course materials, and a PerformOS Certificate of Completion."

> "The PerformOS 12-week AI course is led by an instructor who holds current Microsoft Azure AI certifications including AI-900 and AI-102. These certifications mean the curriculum is built on real, tested knowledge — not guesswork. [Link to sales page]
>
> Microsoft and Azure are trademarks of the Microsoft group of companies. PerformOS is an independent training provider."

- "Microsoft-backed course"

## Testimonial guardrails

Provide to students before they submit:

> "When sharing your experience, please describe your personal learning outcomes. Do not claim the course itself is Microsoft or OpenAI accredited. Safe examples:
> - 'The instructor's Microsoft Azure certifications gave me confidence in the curriculum.'
> - 'I now understand AI concepts I can apply at work.'
> - 'The PerformOS certificate helped me demonstrate my learning to my employer.'"

PerformOS review before publishing:
- Testimonial must not use "Microsoft certified course" or similar
- Testimonial must not use Microsoft, Azure, OpenAI, or ChatGPT logos
- If a testimonial mentions a certification, it must refer to the instructor's credential, not course accreditation
- Reject any testimonial that implies endorsement

## Buyer objection scripts

### "Is this a Microsoft-certified course?"

> "No. PerformOS is an independent training provider. Our course instructor holds current Microsoft Azure AI certifications (AI-900 and AI-102), and those credentials inform the curriculum. But the course itself is not a Microsoft certification program, and completing it does not earn you a Microsoft credential. You receive a PerformOS Certificate of Completion."

### "Does this course prepare me for the AI-900 exam?"

> "The course curriculum is designed by an instructor who holds the AI-900 certification and draws on that knowledge. However, PerformOS does not position this course as exam preparation, and we do not guarantee exam outcomes. If your goal is the AI-900 credential, I recommend supplementing this course with Microsoft's own exam preparation materials."

### "Why doesn't the certificate say Microsoft or Azure?"

> "The PerformOS Certificate of Completion reflects that you have completed a PerformOS program. It is our credential, not Microsoft's. Your instructor's Microsoft certifications are separate — they are the credentials the instructor holds, not credentials the course awards."
